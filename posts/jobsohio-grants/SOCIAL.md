## Animation

`posts/jobsohio-grants/social/size-home-state-cutoffs.gif`
1200x675, 4 frames, 11 seconds, 141 KB.
Built by `scripts/49_social_gif.py`.

The two-by-two as the "small" line moves: 100, then 500, then 5,000 employees
at the parent company, then back to 500 and held five seconds, so the loop
ends on the page default and on the figures the post and the hero publish.

Every frame is a real setting with real values. Nothing is interpolated, so no
frame shows a number that is not in the data. It is the one thing a still
cannot show: "small business" has no single legal meaning, and the share of
the dollars moves from 7.2% to 23% depending on where the line is drawn.

*The 100 and 5,000 figures are not in the post's prose. Like the hero and the
email chart, they are values shown in an image of the tool rather than claims
in copy. Same exception Eric allowed at 2g.*

Alt text:

```
An animated grid crossing business size with home state, showing the share of JobsOhio's grant dollars in each cell as the definition of a small business changes. At under 100 employees, small Ohio companies took 7.2% of the dollars. At under 500, 16%. At under 5,000, 23%. Large companies based outside Ohio fall from 63% to 61% to 52% across the same three settings. Source: JobsOhio Form 990 Schedule I, fiscal years 2014 to 2025.
```

```
"Small business" has no single legal meaning. So we let you move the line.

We spent months on one narrow question: does JobsOhio back small local employers, or large companies from somewhere else?

There is no clean database that gives you business size and headquarters location, so we read 12 years of JobsOhio's IRS Form 990 filings line by line. 2,415 grants. $1,345,318,862. 1,700 companies and organizations. Each one traced to the company that actually owned it at the time.

What we didn't expect was the second dataset that fell out of the first. Going grant by grant, we could also see which companies announced a mass layoff at the funded site afterward, entered bankruptcy, closed the site, or ended the service the grant paid for. We call those failed grants. There are 40 of them, each tied to a document where one exists.

We cannot tell you which grants succeeded. JobsOhio does not routinely publish whether a company met its hiring targets, so there is no success column to put beside that one. We say so in the tool.

The point of this work is to organize and present data that has been hiding in the public domain, so we can all ask better questions about how JobsOhio has performed.

We are not writing any of the stories on these grants today. We built the tool so Ohioans and local reporters can.

#Ohio #EconomicDevelopment #DataJournalism #PublicAccountability
```
