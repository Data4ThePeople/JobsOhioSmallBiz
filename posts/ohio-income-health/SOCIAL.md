## LinkedIn

Attach the map GIF. 1030 characters. The first two lines are the hook.

```
Yesterday we showed who got JobsOhio's grants. Today we look at where the money landed.
Ohio moved economic development out of state government, as if it were separate from public health. The data suggests it may not be.

Across 3,115 Ohio census tracts, the places with lower household income also have more adults in fair or poor health, more diabetes, more smoking and more food insecurity. These are CDC estimates, not counts, and a link like this does not show that one causes the other.

We also mapped 12 years of JobsOhio grants by county on top of tract income. By total dollars, the big circles sit in and around the big cities, where large employers want to locate.

That leaves a question for Ohio: does JobsOhio's current structure allow it to aim economic development at the places where it could also help health?

This is the last post in our JobsOhio series. On Friday we take the income and health question national.

Link in the first comment.
```
